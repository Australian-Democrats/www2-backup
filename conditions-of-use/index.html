What this section is for. All rights in the Party Assets are reserved.

Preventing theft and passing-off. So that the Party Assets are not stolen, misappropriated or used to mislead, you must not — and must not assist, procure or permit any other person to — without our express prior written permission, signed by an authorised officer of the party:

  • copy, reproduce, republish, redistribute, sell or commercially exploit the Party Assets as your own, or strip, alter or obscure our identifying marks;
  • adapt, reverse-engineer, decompile or create competing derivative works from our original designs, components, themes or code;
  • scrape, harvest, mirror, frame or systematically extract the Party Assets, whether manually or by automated means (including to train machine-learning models); or

Your freedom to analyse and criticise is not affected. Robust debate about a political party is a feature of a healthy democracy, not a breach of these terms.

9Security, hacking & unauthorised access

In short: you may use this site as an ordinary visitor. You must not attempt to break into, damage, overload, alter or interfere with the site or the systems behind it. If you notice a security problem, please tell us — but do not test, probe or exploit it. Attempts to hack our systems are strictly prohibited.

The site, its content, its source code and the servers, networks, databases, accounts and infrastructure that run it (together, the “systems”) are provided for lawful, intended use only. You must not — and must not attempt to, assist, procure, encourage or permit any other person to:

  • gain, or try to gain, unauthorised access to any part of the systems, to any account, or to any data, area or functionality that is not intended to be available to you, including by guessing, harvesting, phishing, brute-forcing, reusing, or otherwise obtaining or using another person’s credentials;
  • probe, scan, crawl for weaknesses, port-scan, fuzz, penetration-test, vulnerability-scan or otherwise test the security of the systems, whether manually or with automated tools, without our express prior written permission;
  • circumvent, disable, bypass or defeat any authentication, authorisation, rate-limiting, firewall, filtering, CAPTCHA, or other security, access-control or usage-control measure;
  • introduce, upload, transmit or attempt to install any malware, virus, worm, trojan, ransomware, keylogger, rootkit, logic bomb or any other malicious or harmful code, script or program;
  • inject, tamper with or manipulate data or requests — including by SQL injection, cross-site scripting (XSS), cross-site request forgery, header or parameter tampering, or code injection of any kind;
  • intercept, capture, sniff, or interfere with communications, traffic or data passing to, from or within the systems, or attempt to access data in transit or at rest that is not yours;
  • deface, alter, modify, add to, delete from, corrupt, disrupt or otherwise interfere with the site, its content, its appearance, its code or its operation, or make or distribute any unauthorised modified version of it;
  • impose an unreasonable or disproportionate load on the systems, or carry out any denial-of-service (DoS) or distributed-denial-of-service (DDoS) attack, flooding, or other action intended or likely to disrupt, degrade or disable the site or its availability for others;
  • reverse-engineer, decompile or disassemble any security feature of the systems, or attempt to discover their source code or underlying structure other than as expressly permitted by law; or
  • use the systems to attack, compromise or gain unauthorised access to any other system, network or person.

Reporting a security vulnerability (responsible disclosure). We welcome, and are grateful for, good-faith reports from people who come across a genuine security weakness. If you believe you have found a security vulnerability in this site, please tell us promptly and privately through our contact page, giving enough detail for us to reproduce and fix it, and give us a reasonable opportunity to respond before disclosing it to anyone else. In doing so you must not: access, copy, modify, delete or disclose any data that is not your own; degrade, disrupt or damage the service; use the vulnerability beyond the minimum needed to demonstrate it; or run automated scans or exploits. Reporting a suspected weakness is encouraged and is not itself a breach of these conditions — but this section is not an invitation or authorisation to test, probe, scan or exploit our systems. We do not authorise, and expressly prohibit, any attempt to hack, break into or interfere with our systems, whether or not it is framed as “research”.

We take the security and integrity of our systems, our members’ data and the public’s trust extremely seriously. We monitor for, and log, unauthorised activity. We may suspend or block access, preserve evidence, and report suspected offences to, and fully cooperate with, the Australian Federal Police, the Australian Cyber Security Centre and other authorities. Unauthorised access to, modification of, or impairment of computer data or systems is a criminal offence under the Criminal Code Act 1995 (Cth) and equivalent State and Territory laws, and we will enforce our rights, and pursue recovery of loss, to the fullest extent available.

10National security

In short: our IT team includes cybersecurity specialists, and our systems are monitored and defended. We treat attempted intrusion by foreign state actors as a national security matter: it will be detected, documented and — where lawful and safe to do so — made public, and the hostile state responsible will be named. We stand for Australian national sovereignty and the integrity of our democracy.

Our IT team includes cybersecurity specialists, and our systems are actively monitored and defended around the clock. We treat attempts at intrusion by foreign state actors — or anyone acting on their behalf — with the utmost seriousness. Where it is lawful and consistent with Australia’s national security to do so, any such attempt will be detected, documented, preserved as evidence and made public; the hostile state responsible will be named and called out; and the matter will be referred to the Australian Federal Police, the Australian Cyber Security Centre and the relevant national security agencies. We stand, without qualification, for Australian national sovereignty and the integrity of Australia’s democracy.
